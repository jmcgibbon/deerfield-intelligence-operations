**Question:** How do you coordinate with those teams today—through regular forums/cadences, named counterparts, or primarily ad hoc?

**Question:** As DI grows, which of those interfaces most needs to become more structured?

We can't keep doing ad-hoc "is this okay?" Slacks. We need a structured way to get legal sign-off on data usage and model outputs that actually keeps pace with how fast we’re building. Basically, we need to move away from "Priya pings Sarah from Compliance" to a **standardized workflow**. Whether that’s a dedicated liaison or a clear set of pre-negotiated rules, we just need to stop the one-off fire drills. If we don't fix this, the corporate bureaucracy is going to become the ceiling on how fast we can innovate.

## Scaling

**Question:** As your pod grows, what becomes materially harder to manage?

**Answer:** **Communication and alignment** are the first things to break. With three people, I can just turn my chair around. With ten, I’ll spend my entire day just repeating the same status update to different people. If we don't have a system for sharing information, the "tribal knowledge" model will just lead to massive confusion. **Technical debt and documentation** also become a nightmare. Right now, we can get away with sparse docs because we all know the codebase. But as we add more engineers and more pods, that lack of documentation will become a huge tax. Every new person will be slower to onboard, and we’ll start breaking each other's code because nobody remembers why a certain decision was made six months ago. And finally, **resource competition.** As we grow, the fight for David’s time, for budget, and for the engineers' attention will get way more intense. Without a clear, structured way to prioritize, we’re going to end up with a lot of internal friction and "pod wars." We need to make sure we're still one "Intelligence" team, not just a bunch of silos fighting for the same resources.

**Question:** What works today because the organization is small that won't work at 30?

**Question:** What operational capability or mechanism do you think DI needs to put in place before it feels necessary?

**Question:** We need a **formalized Product Management/Roadmapping function** right now, even if it feels like overkill for ten people. If we wait until we're thirty, we'll already be buried in technical debt and conflicting priorities. We need to start the habit of "planning the work and working the plan" today, so it’s just part of our DNA as we grow. We also need a **centralized "Knowledge Base"**—not just for code, but for our operational processes. How do we onboard a vendor? How do we handle a data privacy issue? If we don't document these "how-to's" now, we're just going to keep reinventing the wheel every time we hire someone new. And honestly, we need a **standardized way to measure impact.** Right now, we "feel" like we're doing well because the analysts are happy, but we don't have real metrics. As we scale, we're going to need to prove our value to the broader firm to justify more headcount and bigger budgets. If we don't start tracking our "wins" and our "ROI" now, we're going to have a hard time defending our resources later. It’s better to build that muscle now while the stakes are lower.

**Question:** Conversely, what do you think we should deliberately keep informal or lightweight as we scale?

**Answer:** The **actual engineering workflows** should stay as lightweight as possible. We don't need heavy "Agile" ceremonies with two-hour planning meetings and rigid story-point estimations. My engineers are pros; they just need to know what the goal is and have the freedom to hit it. Let’s keep the "how we build" part fast and flexible. We should also keep **internal communication** relatively informal. I don't want to move to a world where I have to schedule a formal "sync" just to ask Marcus a quick question. The culture of being able to just grab coffee and solve a problem is one of the best things about being here. We need to add *structure* to the big stuff—like roadmaps and budgets—without killing the *spontaneity* of the day-to-day work. Basically, let’s automate the "boring" stuff (procurement, onboarding, status reporting) so we can keep the "exciting" stuff (brainstorming, coding, problem-solving) as informal and creative as it is today. If we can do that, we’ll be able to scale without losing the soul of the team.
